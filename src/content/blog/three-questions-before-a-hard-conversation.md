Most of us know the feeling. A conversation is coming, and we would rather it were not. The mind rehearses, the body tightens, and we arrive already braced.

What if those minutes before the conversation could become part of the work, rather than only something to get through?

Here are three questions, each drawn from one of the seven levels. There is no need to answer them perfectly. Simply sitting with them can change how we arrive.

## 1. What room does this conversation need? (Space)

Is this the right place? The right time? Is there enough room in us, right now, to listen as well as speak?

Sometimes the most caring thing we can do is to move a conversation: to a quieter place, a slower hour, a day when we are less depleted. That is not avoidance. It is giving the conversation a chance.

## 2. What is this feeling trying to tell me? (Emotions)

Before we speak, we might name what we feel, simply and honestly. *There is worry here. There is some anger here.* Then a second, gentler question: what does this feeling care about?

Often the feeling is pointing toward something important that we have not yet said, even to ourselves.

## 3. What is one true sentence? (Expression)

If the other person could hear only one sentence from us, what would we want it to be? We might write it down, then rewrite it until it is both honest and kind.

> Honest speech that does not wound, and listening that does not wait to reply.

## After the conversation

Whatever happens, we might return to these questions afterward with curiosity rather than judgement. What did we notice? Which level was most alive? What might we try next time?

These three questions are a small taste of the [Seven Levels Framework](/seven-levels/). The [7-Day Conflict Prevention Challenge](/challenge/) offers one reflection like this for each of the seven levels.
